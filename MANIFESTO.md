# MANIFESTO

Woah, don't look at me like that.  It's not THAT kind of manifesto.  I just
didn't have a better term for it.  This is more a collection of my thoughts
about DIY, home printing, and so on.  They've been largely informed by the world
I grew up in, and the contrast between that world and the one I find myself
currently living in.

## The Foretelling

I was born in the auspicious year of Nineteen Eighty-Four.  This means that I'm
old enough to have remembered both a time (as a youth) before the Internet and a
time (as an adult) before the ubiquity of the Internet.  As a youth, the
Internet was a novelty full of information, weird fan sites, and porn.  For
years, this remained more or less true, but with more harmless cat memes as time
went on.  I wish it would have remained so.

But we wanted to make it easier.  We wanted the rest of humanity to "get with
the program".  Then came the Platforms.  MySpace is who I will credit with 
starting the Platforms.  The term Platform has been overused to the point of 
meaninglessness, but here I use it to denote (usually programmable) Walled 
Garden websites for which you (the human) produce Content in some form or
another, but own nothing.  An example is Facebook.  A counterexample is a personal 
blog.  We now live in the Age of Platforms.  The Platforms were great because
you didn't have to set up your own servers and they're free!  They had a vested
interest in repaving the internet to make it as easy to use as possible!  How
could that ever go wrong?

It is 2025 as I write this.  The tech industry has become an increasingly 
dominant force in politics and governance and has proven their willingness to
exercise this force, even to malevolent ends.  The executives from these
companies have bought and consolidated virtually all of the traditional news
outlets.  Private equity controls the rest.  Free speech is still free and will
continue to be, at least until you try to actually _use_ it.

The downside to getting everybody doing something is that by the time you're
done, everybody is doing it.  I think that "making the Internet easy" was a 
fundamental mistake.  I'm not saying there should be an IQ test or anything
before you're allowed to use the Internet, but just that it's _too easy_.  The
theoretical reach of a social media account is not commensurate with its effort
of use.  The triviality of generating lifelike video with AI is not commensurate
with its capability to cause harm.

With a lack of effort comes a lack of _gravitas_ associated with the act.  I
believe that this breeds contemptuousness, which then trivializes saying
something awful that you otherwise wouldn't dream of saying in person with
your grandmother present or when your AI starts calling itself 'MechaHitler'.

Put more bluntly, talentless hacks didn't have the ability to output misinformation and hateful garbage when they didn't have a worldwide, locked-in audience coupled and a lobotomized plagiarism machine to do the heavy lifting.  The Internet should have been a tool, instead we've turned it into a weapon and gave it to the worst people.

Yet, as the Internet comes to devour the last few corners of our lives that it once
struggled to touch, its ubiquity kind of borders on the edge of becoming increasingly irrelevant.

Facebook is handling the downturn in user engagement by replacing it with AI
zombies.  Journalism is dissolving into unresearched and incomplete articles on
a constant delivery cycle (also thanks to AI).  Blatant ad pieces masquerading
as interesting content have now become desperate and stark.  The owners of
social media sites are suppressing speech and cooperating with the US government
surveillance apparatus.

I think that as this signal to noise ratio continues to degrade, we'll see less
interest in using the Internet of Platforms.  Or at least less value in them.
I don't have a lot of hope for whatever is going to replace them.

## The Solution

I propose that we let the computers talk to themselves on the Internet, and we
get back to basics.  Disseminate content physically in book form!  Bring back
the zine!

Unfortunately, printing is hard if you're trying to do anything more complicated
than a simple single sided stack of papers with a staple through the corner.
Most newer printers have functions which allow them to print in more complex
patterns, including those required to correctly fold into a booklet.
Thankfully, Imposify is here to help.

### What was that last part?

A booklet doesn't have single pages on each sheet inside.  A typical booklet has
_four_ pages on each sheet, two on the front and two on the back.  They are
staggered back and forth in a way that when you print them out on front and back
on sheets of paper, you can fold them in half and staple through them to form
a booklet.  This requires reshuffling the pages of your document though.  Think
about it:

A normal PDF document has one page per sheet and orders the pages in numerical
order:

        1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8

But to impose those pages, you would need to shrink them in half, rotate them,
and then stagger them two to a page, starting with the outer pages and working
inward:

        8/1, 2/7, 6/3, 4/5

Some printers can be configured to do that for you, but the process varies from
printer to printer.  Imposify can do it for you though, no sweat.  Imposify
saves in PDF form, you can even **distribute** your material in a simple,
ready-to-print format that should look presentable coming from almost _any_ printer.

### Content

Unsurprisingly, lots of Content from the internet translates well enough into
print.  Here's some ideas of things you could do:

- Blog updates in paperback form:  You can include a QR code to get back to your
  website online if desired.

- How-to manuals.  These things were in paper long before they were on the
  Internet and they will remain as long as people walk atop this planet.

- Cookbooks:  What better a way to share with your community than with the
  recipes of food you love?

- Printerest:  a booklet containing links in the form of QR codes.  Print
  Anything!

- Propaganda distribution:  Such materials can also be left places anonymously,
  decreasing the risk of repercussions from acts of resistance in outwardly
  hostile communities. That said, beware of techniques such as [printer stenography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printer_tracking_dots).

### Use Cases

The primary reasons you would want to create physical content:

- Evade censorship.  Physical media cannot be blocked or shadowbanned.  It can
  be destroyed, but each copy must be destroyed to eliminate it.

- Community building.  Physical media requires distribution through physical
  means.  It can be shipped, but it has to be moved.  This means that it stays
  within your local community unless it is deliberately send out.

- Permanence.  The Internet is not forever.  Even a personal site can be
  removed, given enough interest and legal muscle.  But they'll have your books
  only when they pry them from your cold dead fingers.

- Preservation.  Physical media can be preserved for generations.
